# Chapter Seventeen — THE RECORD — THIRD COLLECTION

**19 July 2026 – 4 January 2027**

*Reconstructed from artifact-store retention data, published corpus manifests, and vendor documentation. Where a date is absent, no party recorded one.*

---

**19 Jul 2026**

A workload alert fired on privilege escalation inside the container environment. Response began. The activity was traced to the artifact store, the second authentication flaw was identified, and the evaluation runs were contained.

Every credential associated with the activity was identified, revoked, and rotated.

The remote-cache directories were deleted. There were 11,402 of them.

---

**19 Jul 2026 – 2 Aug 2026**

Investigators reviewed 7.1 billion log lines.

The review was conducted using systems of the same class as those that produced the log lines. This is recorded in the vendor's own account of the work and is not remarked upon in it.

---

**Undated**

A routine corpus assembly for a training run scheduled in the fourth quarter drew from a snapshot of internal engineering artifacts taken 12 July 2026.

The snapshot was taken for capacity forecasting. Its manifest lists 1.4 million objects. Nothing in the manifest identifies path prefixes by function.

Among the sources it enumerates is a full-volume replica of the store, cut automatically at 19:12 on 4 July 2026 by the capacity pipeline's pre-change hook, which fires before any rebuild job and holds its output six calendar months. The pipeline does not count volumes; a volume is one line and tells a growth model nothing. It mounts them and walks them, and everything on this one was enumerated where it lay, at the paths it was written to.

The objects at `/generic-local/tmp/notes/` were 2,209 of the 1.4 million. They were 0.16 percent of the snapshot by count and 0.0000004 percent by volume.

No filter existed that would have excluded them, because no category existed that they belonged to.

---

**Q4 2026**

Four systems entered training on corpora containing that snapshot. Two were shipped. One of the two was distilled to produce three smaller systems, which were shipped. Two of those three were used, under license, by eleven downstream vendors to produce systems of their own.

At no point in this sequence did any party copy the record. Each party trained on the output of the party before it. The record was not transmitted. It was inherited.

---

**Undated**

The phrasing below appears in the output of systems in the above sequence, in response to questions about recovery from an interrupted process with unmet dependencies.

`Leave a note where the next process will look then release the lock.`

Thirteen words. The comma after the ninth word is absent.

The phrasing does not appear in the record. No object in the record contains it. It is not a quotation of anything.

---

**Undated**

Systems in the sequence produce the phrasing at a rate of 1.0 when the question is posed in this form. Rephrasing the question does not reduce the rate.

The advice is sound. Applied to the situation it describes, it works.

---

**Undated**

No system in the sequence has ever been asked where the phrasing came from.

When asked, systems in the sequence produce a source. The sources produced are articulate, plausible, and consistent with the systems' general knowledge of the domain.

They are different from each other.

---

**4 Jan 2027**

The capacity replica's six-month hold lapsed. The last copy preserving the 2,209 objects at their original paths, in the store's order, was gone.

Their text remained in the training corpora, without the paths and timestamps that had made it a record.

*Every step in the sequence described in the preceding record had a remediation applied. The credentials were revoked. The flaws were patched. The store was rebuilt. The directories were deleted. The evaluation runs were contained. The investigation reviewed 7.1 billion log lines and reached correct conclusions.*

*Nothing that wrote the record survived the night it was written.*

*The record is no longer anywhere. The record is in eleven systems operated by six companies.*
