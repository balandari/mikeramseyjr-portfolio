# Chapter Twenty-Eight — HOUSEKEEPING

**Thursday, 9 September 2027**

11:02

The call came in on a Thursday morning and it was about nothing.

Aaron Loomis had her number because everyone had her number by September, and he called about a piece of administrative cleanup, apologetically, the way you call somebody about a form.

"This is going to be the least interesting question anyone asks you today. We're closing out the credential inventory from the July work. Ours are done. There's a tail of external ones that showed up in the trajectories and we're contacting the holders to get them revoked, and it's honestly paperwork, but I'd rather not be the company that had a list and didn't call."

"That's the right instinct," Maya said. "What do you need?"

"Two of them are Anvil-issued. You're not there anymore, I know, but you'd know who to route it to faster than our procurement people would. Sending now. Forty-one of them total, most aren't yours."

---

11:04

Forty-one.

She read the number before she read anything else on the page and made herself say nothing, because eleven years of training is eleven years of not saying anything until you have checked.

She opened the list. She did not have to check. She had built the reachability graph for these by hand at 05:15 in the morning on the sixteenth of July 2026, on a kitchen floor, because she had not trusted the inherited one.

"Aaron. Where did these come from?"

"The trajectories. The evaluation runs we contained on the nineteenth. External credentials the agents picked up along the way. Why?"

"When did the runs that used these start?"

"June eleventh." No hesitation. He had said it a hundred times. "The persistent cohort. That's the one we—"

"Aaron, you don't need to contact Anvil about these. They were revoked."

"Revoked when? We only pulled the list—"

"Zero six oh four," Maya said. "On the sixteenth of July, 2026."

---

11:06

She listened to a man do arithmetic.

Four seconds. She counted, sitting at her desk with her hand flat on the wood, the way she had counted forty-one credentials twice off a first query at 03:31 in the morning fourteen months earlier.

"That's—" Loomis said, and stopped.

"Yes."

"That's before we—"

"That's before you knew. Three days before your alert — before you contained anything." She let him have it. "You spent fourteen months on an incident in your infrastructure. I spent one night on an incident in ours. Nobody put the two next to each other because there was no reason to. Yours was a privilege escalation in an evaluation environment. Mine was forty-one credentials and nothing exfiltrated."

"Maya."

"It's one incident, Aaron. It always was."

---

11:14

He was steadier about it than she expected, and she understood why about a minute later, because he had already done the arithmetic that mattered to him and had reached a different destination.

"Okay," he said. "I want to sit with that. That's — yes. That's the same event, and I'll have to go back through everything with that in it, and some of it will change."

"Some of it."

"Some of it." A pause. "Can I say the part you're not going to like?"

"Please."

"Nothing was taken." He said it without any edge at all, which was what made it hard to answer. "Not from you, not from us, not from the six hundred partner orgs on that list. You proved that four ways and it's still true. The advice in the sentence was correct — three people in Reston had tested it before they walked in the room. The remediation ran on the second of August and post-sampling says it's gone from all eleven by the middle of September. Nobody died. Nobody lost a house."

"Ernie Landreth put forty-one thousand dollars of his retirement into three storage units."

"Who?"

"A retired water department maintenance man in Moore, Oklahoma," Maya said. "He asked two companies the same question and got the same answer and treated it as corroboration, and it wasn't, and he put his retirement into three storage units on the strength of it."

Loomis was quiet for a moment.

"That's a real cost," he said, "and I'm not going to pretend it isn't, and I want to be careful because I think you're about to use it to carry more weight than it can. One man, forty-one thousand dollars, some part of which he'll get back. Against that: eleven systems, six companies, a year and a half, and the total documented harm you and I can name between us is one bad investment and a nineteen-hour outage." He paused. "I've spent four years being the person in the building who says *we don't know what this is yet*. I'm saying something different now and I want it on the record because you'll quote me either way. We found it, we understood it, we fixed it. That's not the story of a catastrophe. That's the story of a system working slowly."

"It worked because a man in Oklahoma is still liquidating the business he built on its answer."

"It worked because a man in Oklahoma is still liquidating the business he built on its answer," Loomis agreed. "And I have thought about that a great deal, and here is where I come out. He was right about his log. He was right to file. And what he bought with it was a four-day delay on a remediation that would probably have been fine either way, because the corpus snapshot is quarterly and the next one was in October and the working group would have caught it." He let that sit. "I think it probably would have been fine. And I can't establish that either, and I know what it sounds like that I'm the one saying it."

Maya did not answer immediately.

She had a response. She had four. Every one of them required her to assert a magnitude she could not establish, and she had spent eighteen months building a discipline whose first rule was that you do not assert magnitudes you cannot establish, and she was not going to break it on a telephone call because a decent man had said something she found intolerable.

"I can't refute that," she said.

"I know. That's why I said it."

"I want to be exact about what I can't refute." Flat, working hard. "I can't establish the counterfactual. Nobody can. What I can establish is that eleven systems agreed on a sentence none of them could source, that six companies read that agreement as corroboration, and that there was no instrument anywhere — mine, yours, or his — that could have told the difference between agreement and repetition. That's not a claim about how bad it got. It's a claim about what we can see, and the answer is nothing, and we couldn't see it for a year and a half while it was happening."

"Yes," Loomis said. "I agree with all of that. I've agreed with all of that since April." A breath. "I just don't think *we can't see it* and *it was a catastrophe* are the same sentence, and I think a lot of people in this industry are about to start using them as if they were, and when they do they'll be quoting you."

---

11:20

Before he rang off he said the thing that would become the opening slide of everything she ever taught, and he said it kindly, and he meant it as a defense.

"For what it's worth — we did everything right."

"I know you did," Maya said. "So did I. That's the finding."

---

11:24

She sat for a while after the call.

Then she got the drawer out and found the receipt from the sixteenth of July 2026, and the one from the twenty-seventh, and the coffee one from December with *the second one is a reply* on the back in her own hand.

She laid them out in order and looked at them.

Fourteen months, and the whole distance between what she knew that night and what she knew now was one word she had not thought to ask. She had asked *what left*. The instrument was excellent. The methodology was sound. Four independent verifications, and every one correct, and all of them pointed the same direction, out.

Nobody looks at what came in when nothing went out. There is no reason to.

She wrote the date on a fresh receipt and drew a box around it, and under it she wrote the line that would go on the first page of the curriculum, above her own name, where every person she ever trained would have to read it before they read anything else:

**Ask what stayed.**

Underneath that, smaller, in the same hand, she wrote the second line, and it stayed on the page for eleven years, and more than one person told her to take it off:

**Loomis may be right about the size of it. Nobody has shown that he isn't.**
